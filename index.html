Research proposal, EGH404 Research in Engineering Practice, 2026

Where single-phase cooling runs out

Single-phase liquid cooling is validated to 52 kW per rack. Next-generation AI racks need 180 to 220 kW. I proposed a three-scale experiment to measure where single-phase stops being enough, and whether two-phase cooling extends the limit.

Gap
No study compares both modes on one boundary above package scale
Method
270 controlled runs, an 8-branch rack-slice, then a live rack
Preview
Single-phase limit of 1.13 kW per GPU at the worst case, on synthetic data

EGB316 Design of Machine Elements, individual, 2026

Weld fatigue design audit

A fatigue audit of the welded rear hanger on a 750 kg trailer, from load path to ANSYS validation. Hand calculation and FEA both predict infinite life, and I traced the 2.2 times gap between them to three causes.

Fatigue FoS
6.33 by hand, 2.86 in ANSYS
Mesh
Converged at 41.5 MPa with a 0.1 mm weld-toe mesh
Finding
The 5 mm weld is about 2.5 times larger than a safety factor of 2 requires

About

I am a third-year Mechanical Engineering (Honours) student at QUT with an aerospace minor. I work from first principles: I break a system into its load paths, failure modes and energy flows, and check the answer by hand before I trust FEA or CAD.

My interests run along two lines. One is Australian sovereign defence capability and the engineering behind platforms like the MQ-28A Ghost Bat. The other is the physical limits now shaping AI infrastructure: power density, cooling and grid capacity. Both come down to the same physics of heat, flow and structure.

Outside coursework I was one of two aerostructures leads on a QUT Aerospace Society UAV, taking it from a blank sheet to a design-and-sizing milestone finalised in CAD.
